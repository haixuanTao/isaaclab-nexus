The task layer, measured

Isaac Lab's manager-based MDP — observation terms, reward terms, resets — compiles to chains of small tensor ops. Comparing each family's share of launches against its share of work isolates what that costs.

The finding

662,324 launches — 51% of every kernel launch in the run — produce 6.2% of the GPU work, averaging 2.1 µs each. The task layer contributes almost no arithmetic and roughly half the dispatch pressure.

Power, not utilization

At 4,096 envs the card draws 182 W of a 500 W cap — 36% — while nvidia-smi reports 60% "utilization." That metric counts the fraction of time at least one kernel is resident, not occupancy or throughput. High utilization at low power is the signature of many small serialized kernels.

8,192 environments fails twice

Out of the box, PhysX overflows its GPU broadphase buffer:

PxGpuDynamicsMemoryConfig::foundLostAggregatePairsCapacity
  needs 172,733,764 — default is 2**25 (33,554,432)
  "otherwise, the simulation will miss interactions"

The run took 55.2 s per iteration and was silently dropping contacts. Raising the buffer 8× made it slower — 68.35 s — because PhysX then actually computes what it had been discarding. Throughput against 4,096 envs collapses from 24,638 to 2,876 env-steps/s: an 8.6× loss for a 2× batch increase.

The cause is the task configuration, not PhysX. STAND_UP_ROUGH_TERRAIN_G1_CFG generates a fixed grid of 72 tiles at 8 × 8 m, independent of num_envs. More environments pack more robots onto the same tiles:

EnvsRobots per 8 × 8 m tileArea per robot
1,024144.5 m²
4,096571.1 m²
8,1921140.56 m²

At 8,192 the robots stand shoulder-to-shoulder, so broadphase aggregate pairs grow superlinearly. Scaling past ~4,096 requires growing the terrain grid with the environment count — which no CLI flag does, and which changes the task.

Read it this way

The claim these numbers support is not "faster." It is that the two stacks fail differently. AGILE leaves the GPU idle half the time and spends 6.1% of its kernel time on a task layer that issues half its launches. Zealot's draft reports a denser GPU and a task layer of 0.2%.

The comparison becomes controlled only when both columns are taken on one machine at one environment count with a stated solver budget. Both are real today; they are just not yet the same experiment.

Two places a reader will stumble

The rollout family total does not follow from the phase table. The draft's families read "rollout 850, update 190, reset + GAE 20" = 1,060, against 966 ms reported as accounted. Summing the phase table's own rollout rows (646 + 41 + 38 + 14 + 12 + 5) gives 756, and 756 + 190 + 20 = 966 exactly. Either 850 is a typo for 756, or the families are wall spans that include idle the phase rows don't attribute — in which case rollout's span exceeds the sum of its parts by 94 ms and the two tables are measuring different things. Both are defensible; the paper needs to say which, because 1,060 ms of non-overlapping work cannot fit a 1,000 ms iteration.

"82% GPU" and "75% GPU busy" are not a contradiction, though they read as one. 796 ms is wall time spent in GPU-side phases; 747.25 ms is actual kernel execution. The 48.75 ms difference is GPU idle inside those phases — they are 93.9% dense. Both statements are true of different quantities; naming which is which in the caption removes the apparent conflict.

08

What this measurement cannot say

  • No CPU sampling. The container reports perf_event_paranoid=4 with perf_event_open unavailable, so nsys CPU sampling, context-switch tracing and --gpu-metrics are all unavailable. CUPTI tracing is unaffected. Host-side attribution therefore stops at the CUDA API — there are no CPU stacks behind it.
  • Power is polled, not traced. Sampled from nvidia-smi at 50–100 ms, not from GPU hardware counters.
  • No NVTX ranges. AGILE ships none, so phase boundaries were recovered from kernel composition and cross-checked against rsl_rl's timers rather than read from markers.
  • Profiling overhead ≈7%. Iteration 4.28 s traced against 3.99 s untraced; the ratios above are unaffected, the absolute times are inflated.
  • One GPU, one task, one seed. A 500 W RTX 5090 — figures quoted against a 600 W part are not comparable.
  • DOF caveat. Every G1 task simulates the full 29-DOF articulation; the "12 DOF" variants differ only in action-space width. A 12-vs-29 comparison measures policy width, not simulation cost.
  • The Zealot column was measured elsewhere. It is the authors' own profiling data, transcribed from the draft — real, but taken on a different machine at an unstated environment count, so it is not controlled against the AGILE column.
  • Solver budgets are not matched. AGILE runs PhysX TGS with 8 position and 4 velocity iterations per substep, 4 substeps per control step — 32 position and 16 velocity iterations per control step. TGS re-linearizes contacts each position iteration, so its iteration count is not interchangeable with a Gauss-Seidel count. Matching solvers on iteration count compares unlike units; match on an outcome — penetration depth, constraint residual, or task-level tracking error at equal wall-time — and report the counts as configuration.
